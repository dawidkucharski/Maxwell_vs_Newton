GAP 1: [MAJOR] GAP-AN-001
Category: missing_anisotropy_analysis
Confidence: 0.90
Domains affected: stylus_profilometry, optical_interferometry, surface_roughness_psd

STATEMENT:
  No anisotropy tensor formulation exists for surface metrology instruments. Stylus profilometry is inherently anisotropic (raster scanning along x), while optical systems have different NA-limited resolution in x and y. The literature characterises surface anisotropy but NOT measurement-system anisotropy. An anisotropy tensor A_ij = ∫ f_i f_j H(f) df could quantify directional information loss.

EVIDENCE:
  • Surface anisotropy studied: True
  • Instrument anisotropy characterised: 0 papers
  • Zero papers found proposing an anisotropy tensor for measurement systems

SUGGESTED APPROACH:
  Define A_ij = ∫ f_i f_j H_sensor(f_x, f_y) df_x df_y, compute eigenvalues λ_1, λ_2 quantifying principal directions of information loss.

GAP 2: [CRITICAL] GAP-IT-001
Category: missing_information_theoretic_formulation
Confidence: 0.85
Domains affected: all

STATEMENT:
  No information-theoretic formulation exists for surface metrology. Measurement can be viewed as an information channel: the surface h(x,y) is the source, the instrument is the channel, and the measured topography is the received signal. Information loss I(f_x, f_y) = log(PSD_true/PSD_measured) provides a spectral map of where each instrument loses information. This framework is entirely absent from the surface metrology literature.

EVIDENCE:
  • Information-theoretic concepts in metrology literature: False
  • Zero papers frame stylus/optical comparison as information channel problem

SUGGESTED APPROACH:
  Define I(f_x, f_y) = log(PSD_true / PSD_measured), compute mutual information between true and measured surfaces, derive channel capacity bounds.

GAP 4: [MAJOR] GAP-FD-001
Category: missing_frequency_domain_interpretation
Confidence: 0.55
Domains affected: surface_roughness_psd, optical_interferometry, stylus_profilometry

STATEMENT:
  The frequency-domain interpretation of measurement distortion is incomplete. While PSD analysis is widely used for surface characterisation, the PSD of the measurement operator itself — H_sensor(f_x, f_y) — is rarely computed or used to correct raw topography data. Mack (2013, 2015) identified systematic PSD errors in lithography contexts, but this has not been generalised to surface metrology instrument comparison.

EVIDENCE:
  • PSD used in 2 papers
  • PSD applied to instrument characterisation: 5 papers
  • No instrument transfer function PSD comparison between modalities found

SUGGESTED APPROACH:
  Compute H_sensor(f) = PSD_measured(f) / PSD_true(f) for both modalities, analyse where each system loses information in k-space.

GAP 5: [CRITICAL] GAP-EQ-001
Category: missing_equivalence_regimes_between_sensors
Confidence: 0.50
Domains affected: stylus_profilometry, optical_interferometry

STATEMENT:
  No equivalence regime analysis in k-space exists between stylus and optical profilometry. The question 'Under what spatial-frequency conditions do these modalities produce equivalent measurements?' is unanswered. An equivalence regime could be defined where |H_stylus - H_optical| < ε in some k-space region, providing practitioners with rigorous criteria for instrument interchangeability.

EVIDENCE:
  • Cross-modality comparison papers: 8
  • Equivalence conditions discussed: False
  • No k-space equivalence criterion defined in any paper

SUGGESTED APPROACH:
  Define k-space equivalence region: Ω_eq = {(f_x, f_y) : |H_s - H_o| < 0.1}. Find scaling of Ω_eq area with tip radius, NA, and roughness parameters.

GAP 6: [MAJOR] GAP-OP-001
Category: missing_unified_operator_framework
Confidence: 0.40
Domains affected: stylus_profilometry, optical_interferometry

STATEMENT:
  No unified k-space operator model exists for stylus vs optical profilometry that accounts for anisotropic information loss. The literature treats these modalities independently, without a common mathematical framework relating measurement operators M_stylus[h(x,y)] and M_optical[h(x,y)] in the frequency domain.

EVIDENCE:
  • Only 26 papers address both stylus and optical modalities
  • Operator formalism found: False
  • Transfer function approach found: True
  • Systematic modality comparison beyond RMSE: True

SUGGESTED APPROACH:
  Define M_s[h] = T_s * h + n_s and M_o[h] = F^{-1}[H_o · F[h]] + n_o, analyse their ratio in k-space: H_ratio(f_x, f_y) = H_o/H_s
