Surface Topography: Metrology and Properties}

\opening{Dear Editor,}

Please consider the manuscript ``Optical Profilometry Versus the Stylus: A Spectral Theory for Closing the Accuracy Gap'' for publication as a research paper in \emph{Surface Topography: Metrology and Properties}.

\textbf{Scope fit.} The journal's core remit is the measurement and characterisation of surface topography, and the manuscript addresses it at the instrument-physics level. Stylus and optical profilometry are represented as $k$-space transfer operators within one consistent computational framework, which quantifies where the two modality families agree, where they diverge, and why---and prescribes concrete instrument improvements that close the gap (equivalence over \SI{11.5}{\percent}$\pm$\SI{0.3}{\percent} of the noise-supported $k$-space at baseline, expandable to \SI{82.4}{\percent}). A Shannon-theoretic formulation adds fundamental reconstruction limits, and the optical channel is benchmarked against a public measured WLI transfer-function dataset (Hirose et al.\ 2025, CC-BY-4.0). The results are directly usable by metrologists and instrument developers---the journal's core readership---and every number is reproducible from the committed code.

All results are computational; the supplementary material (32~pages) carries the extended derivations, scatterometry extension, six-class stress test, and GUM uncertainty analysis. Code, configuration, and data are public (GitHub release v1.2.0) and archived on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23615499). The manuscript is a single-author study submitted with full author details; it has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.
